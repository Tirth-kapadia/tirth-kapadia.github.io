Neurological and Thermodynamic Mechanisms Underlying Anomalous Presence Perception

A unified mechanistic explanation for the universal experience of sensing a deceased person's presence — proposing infrasound, predictive processing failure, and Neural Persistence as interacting biological causes.

Neuroscience Predictive Processing Bereavement Thermodynamics Neural Persistence
Abstract

This hypothesis explores the neurological and thermodynamic mechanisms underlying anomalous presence perception, the experience of sensing a deceased person's presence. Among the most universally documented phenomena in human history, reported across all cultures, all eras, and all psychological profiles, it has never received a unified scientific explanation. This hypothesis proposes that anomalous presence perception is not supernatural, not pathological, and not imagined, but a precise biological consequence of five interacting mechanisms: infrasound induced cortical arousal operating below conscious sensory threshold, predictive processing failure during bereavement, Neural Persistence, the continued firing of long term potentiated neural pathways built around a specific individual, Cardiac Neural Persistence, the encoding of relational memory in the heart's own intrinsic nervous system generating somatic presence independently of cortical reconstruction, and the Negativity Threshold, a proposed residual signature left by sustained self hate, regret, and chronic stress that a visitor's subconscious can detect as mismatch even without any personal relationship to the deceased. Version 3 extends the framework in three directions: the Negativity Threshold mechanism itself, aimed at stranger reported experiences with no personal relational encoding; a distinction between actionable and unactionable threat, proposing that the disorientation and loss of reality testing reported during presence experiences comes from the absence of a defensive action available to the subconscious, not from the intensity of the threat response itself; and a resolution of the apparent conflict between predictive hallucination and direct sensory detection.
